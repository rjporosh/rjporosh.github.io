Signal

about

I've spent close to five years writing production C# — most of it with ASP.NET Core, since the early days of .NET Core in 2017, through to .NET 8 and current releases. The bulk of that time has gone into backend systems that don't get to fail quietly: REST APIs, microservices, and message-driven architecture built on SQL Server, MySQL, Redis, and RabbitMQ.

The project I'm proudest of is an international payment gateway processing more than a million transactions a day. Building at that scale is where I learned to actually care about concurrency, fault tolerance, and transaction consistency — not as interview terms, but as the difference between a good night and an on-call page.

Outside of the day job, I'm working through applied AI and ML — mostly looking for where they can make an engineering team faster without adding fragility. I care more about solving the right problem cleanly than about the framework used to solve it.

At this volume, small inefficiencies compound fast — a query that's fine at ten requests a second isn't fine at ten thousand. Most of my time on this system went into making failure predictable: retries that don't duplicate a charge, queues that survive a downstream outage, and APIs that degrade instead of falling over.
